Lab notes for Tesseract OCR for Mere Mortals
=============================================

Machine: small Linux sandbox (Ubuntu 24.04). Engine: Tesseract
5.3.4 with Leptonica 1.82.0, from Ubuntu's tesseract-ocr
5.3.4-1build5 packages. The sandbox's package manager was held
by other work throughout, so the .deb files were unpacked into
a scratch folder and the identical binary run from there;
languages eng, deu, fra plus osd were added the same way.

Fixtures: every page was generated programmatically (PIL) from
invented texts — a community-hall cleanup notice, a bakery
receipt, a two-column gardening article, a German reading-circle
notice, a single line, a single word. Damaged variants were made
from the clean notice: skew 3 and 8 degrees, speckle (mild and
heavy, with blotches), pale ink (two levels, one blurred),
uneven light (gradient and hard shadow), low resolution (about
100 and about 70 dpi), no DPI metadata, upside down.
make-images.py and make-images-hard.py are the generators;
fix-images.py performs the repairs; measure.py computes the
character error rate against the known truths.

Headline measurements (character accuracy, whitespace-normalised):
clean 100.0; skew 3deg 99.6, skew 8deg 38.0 -> deskew 100.0;
speckle mild 96.9, heavy 83.8 -> median+threshold 93.2;
pale ink 100.0 (both levels); uneven light 100.0, hard shadow
97.9 -> normalise+threshold 95.8 (the fix lost); lowres ~100dpi
99.6, ~70dpi 87.5 -> upscale 3x 99.2; no-DPI 100.0 ("Estimating
resolution as 402"); upside down 14.7 -> --psm 1 100.0; German
notice under eng 99.2 (the umlaut), under deu 100.0, under
eng+deu 99.2. Receipt measured 73.6 but a diff showed the only
differences were two dashed rules — every word and figure right.

Surprises worth the reader's trust: the engine is much tougher
on contrast problems than folklore says, much weaker on tilt
past a few degrees; --psm 8 failed on the lab's single word
twice while the default read it; and a searchable PDF's text
layer, pulled back with pdftotext, measured 98.1 against the
plain text's 100.0 — the difference is the extractor's word
order, not the OCR's reading.
